The Initiative

Built by the community, for the community.

This platform is a collective Iraqi Biradari initiative created to preserve family history, community records, rare documents, public announcements, events, videos, and welfare-related updates in a simple and accessible digital form.

The work is carried forward by community members who contribute knowledge, documents, family references, meeting notes, technical coordination, and voluntary support. Some contributors help identify and verify older material, some organize archives, some coordinate public communication, and others support the welfare and membership effort.

The goal is not only to digitize information, but to make the community's memory easier to find, easier to trust, and easier to pass on to future generations.

People Behind the Effort

Welfare & Support Coordinators

Platform Principle

The Shajra remains isolated from the public content system so lineage research can evolve safely.

What this platform is responsible for

The website is being developed as a public gateway for community-facing material: announcements, documents, videos, events, support information, and links to the separate genealogy explorer.

The effort aims to keep information organized, respectful, and easy to update. Over time, the archive can grow through verified submissions, admin-published posts, curated documents, and community participation.

Public support and membership communication is routed through the Support page, while lineage exploration continues separately through the Shajra.

Source-Backed History

The historical overview below is summarized and adapted from the Wikipedia article on Iraqi Biradari, with source credit preserved.

Identity and settlement

Iraqi Biradari is described as a Sunni Muslim community found chiefly in the Ghazipur, Azamgarh, Ballia, Deoria, and Gorakhpur districts of eastern Uttar Pradesh, India. The community is also referred to as Iraqi Shaikh.

The article records Urdu, Hindi, Bhojpuri, and English among the languages associated with the community, with Islam and Sunni practice listed as its religious identity.

History and origin

Wikipedia summarizes Iraqi Biradari as a descendant population of immigrants from Iraq, with later settlement in Ghazipur during the Delhi Sultanate period. The article connects this history with Sayyid Masud Al Hussaini and his family, and notes a broad community origin roughly seven centuries ago.

It also notes that older family lines are preserved in distinct family trees across several towns and villages. This is why the public heritage site and the separate Shajra explorer belong together as companion efforts, while remaining technically separate.

Migration and wider presence

The article mentions historic Iraqi populations in eastern Uttar Pradesh, including Lar, and notes that Iraqi families also migrated to Karachi, Pakistan after Indian independence in 1947.

This archive will continue to preserve public community material, documents, meeting records, books, and oral memory so that historical identity and family knowledge remain easier to find.
